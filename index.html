          Method

          A fly, a smell, and a memory.

          The circuit

          The mushroom body is the fruit fly's centre for learning what smells good or bad. We take every neuron of it from the FlyWire whole-brain connectome — 277 olfactory projection neurons, 5,177 Kenyon cells, the APL and DPM neurons, 323 dopamine neurons and 96 output neurons — wired by their 1.55 million real synapses, signed by predicted neurotransmitter.

          The translation

          A fly knows nothing of jobs or parties, but its world has ripe fruit, fermenting fruit, other flies, mould, the CO₂ of stressed flies, safe places to lay eggs, and green leaves. Each of these is detected by specific olfactory glomeruli, identified by their receptors in the literature (for example, geosmin from harmful mould is detected only by DA2). Your option's words are matched to up to two of these things and their glomeruli are switched on; the rest of the eight-glomerulus odour is drawn from the name, so every option still smells like itself. Options that mean similar things share glomeruli, so the fly finds them harder to tell apart. The dictionary is ours; what each glomerulus detects is not.

          The encoding

          Your answers decide what the smell is worth. Upside drives the PAM dopamine neurons, risk and effort drive the PPL1 dopamine neurons, newness sets odour intensity, familiarity sets learning strength, unpredictability adds trial-to-trial jitter. This model has the mushroom body only, so a fly's inborn reactions to a smell are not simulated: the meaning shapes which neurons carry the option, and the answers teach the fly what it is worth.

          The dynamics

          A firing-rate model runs in two phases. Learning: each odour is smelled while dopamine teaches the circuit, weakening Kenyon-cell synapses onto output neurons — the fly's real learning rule. Test: each odour is smelled again; output neurons that promote approach are weighed against those that promote avoidance. Which outputs mean "approach" is derived from the wiring, not assigned by hand.

          The colours

          Each population has one colour everywhere: in the brain, the charts and the text. A neuron lights up only when the engine computed a firing rate for it.

          What it is not

          No language model makes the choice. The fly does not understand jobs, people, cities or money. This is an experimental instrument and a creative tool, not advice. Ignore the fly. It's a fly.

          Where it runs

          The whole simulation runs on your own device, in your browser or in the app. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
